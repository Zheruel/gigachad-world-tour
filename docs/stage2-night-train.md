# STAGE 2 — THE NIGHT TRAIN

One route, 9000 logical px, **18.75 screens**, about **15 minutes**. A station at nine in the
evening and a sleeper heading south at midnight. The level is about *leaving*: the first third is
the only stretch in the game where CHAD is trying to be somewhere rather than to win a fight, and
the last two thirds are the only arena in the game that moves.

## The areas

| | Area | x | Length | The one idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **the forecourt and ticket hall** | 0–1600 | ~3 min | the crowd is furniture: half speed unless you dash |
| 2 | **the footbridge** | 1600–2400 | ~2 min | a drop onto the tracks either side — the pit, vertical |
| 3 | **the parcel dock** | 2400–3400 | ~2 min | trolleys running loose down a slope |
| 4 | **platform 1** | 3400–4600 | ~2½ min | the board flips, the rake is in, and it leaves without you |
| 5 | **the carriages** | 4600–7200 | ~3½ min | 30 px of depth, a lurch, a tunnel, a chain to pull |
| 6 | **the roof** | 7200–9000 | ~2½ min | 80 px of depth, wind, and low bridges |

**The join is a cut, not a walk** — the only one in the game. Area 4 ends with a running jump
into a moving door and area 5 opens in the vestibule with the platform lights sliding away. One
second of black, and the world outside starts moving.

## What the train does

**The corridor squeezes the depth lane** from 60 px to **30**. Half the depth you have fought in
for a whole level: sidestepping stops being an answer, everything goes lateral, and enemies queue
instead of surrounding you. On the roof it opens to **80**, wider than anywhere in the game, and
the release is enormous.

**The lurch.** On curves the carriage tilts and everything on the floor — both sides — slides
toward one wall for 50 frames. Telegraphed by the scenery before it reaches you: the hanging
chains swing first, then the tea glasses slide, then you do.

**The windows run on their own clock**, right to left, nothing to do with the camera: black
fields, a level crossing with a line of waiting scooters and one impatient headlight, a station
gone in a second and a half, and once, a goods train that fills every window and *roars*. Two are
hazards — the **tunnel** blacks the carriage to emergency lights so you fight by silhouette, and
the **girder bridge** strobes hard shadows at 6 Hz.

**The chain.** A **runner** — the same wave flag the dabbawala used — sprints for the emergency
chain instead of fighting. If he reaches it the train brakes: everyone in the carriage slides
forward and goes down, and the next wave arrives early. The chain is a **breakable**, one per
carriage, above head height and easy to miss. Break it yourself and that never happens again.

## The cast

**One new type, arriving where it belongs.** The dredging crew followed him to the station, and
seeing the same enemies in a railway hall is the story being told without a line of dialogue.
What changes is what they are *doing* and what room they are doing it in — plus MANJA, whose
whole job is being above the fighting lane, and a sleeper carriage has a shelf of them:

| | Here | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| **GOONDA** | in a crowd you cannot see through; later, the runner going for the chain | unchanged, and that is the point |
| **BATTA** | swinging in a ticket hall full of pillars | bait the arc into the pillars and his own side |
| **BANDAR** | **he goes for the ticket, not the food** | kill him before he reaches the screen edge |
| **THELA** | a **steel trunk** on his head instead of a cart; then the same man in 30 px of corridor | break the trunk — and know the spill costs you the lane |
| **COOKER** | a station tea urn, then the pantry kettle | close the gap, and remember he vents when he dies |
| **MANJA** *(new)* | **the upper berths** — he never comes down until you make him | jump-attack him, or wait for the drop and punish |

**The ticket.** BANDAR has spent a whole level stealing food off the floor; here he takes the one
thing that is not on the floor. If he reaches the screen edge with it, nothing happens — no
message, no penalty, no fail state — and it comes back forty minutes later when the TTE puts his
hand out. It is the only consequence in the game that outlives the level it was set in, and it
works precisely because nothing announces it.

**One heavy, three props so far.** Handcart, boat pole, steel trunk. Same rig, same lesson, three
silhouettes — and THELA in a 30 px corridor genuinely is a different enemy from THELA on an open
platform, which is a difference that costs nothing.

## MINIBOSS — THE DEPARTURE

**A situation, not a person**, and the only one in the game.

The whistle goes at x 4300, the rake starts at 0.4 px/f and accelerates, and the platform runs
out at 4600. Waves arrive from both ends while the open door slides away from you: standing still
to finish a group is a real cost, and so is running past one and taking hits in the back. It ends
with a running jump.

Miss it and it is a **soft fail**, never a game over: you sprint the last of the platform, catch
the guard's van instead, and the carriages open one coach further back with two extra men in it.

Nothing else in the chapter ends this way, and it is why this level is about leaving rather than
winning.

## MID-BOSS — THE TTE

Black coat, badge, torch, a ledger under one arm, reading glasses. **"TICKET?"**

He is not corrupt. He is not even angry. He is the rules, and the joke is that this makes him the
hardest thing on the train.

- **the torch** (green, reflectable) — a beam down the corridor that blinds for 30 frames.
  Reflect it and *he* is blind, which is the only free window you get.
- **the ledger** (green, counterable) — flat, fast, short.
- **THE CHECK** — the mechanic. He walks to a downed enemy, stamps the ledger over him, and
  **that enemy gets back up with half health**. Every ~7 seconds, forever. The fight is
  unwinnable until you accept that he is the target and everyone else is weather.
- If the bandar took your ticket at the footbridge, he opens hostile with two extra men.
- No phase flip, no rage. At zero he adjusts his glasses, notes something down, and gets off at
  the next station.

The arena is the vestibule: 30 px of depth, a door hanging open on the rushing dark, the bellows
flexing between coaches. Nowhere to go, which is the point.

## LEVEL BOSS — BIRJU, THE COUPLER

A railway shunter on the roof of a moving train at midnight. A coupling chain over one shoulder, a
shunting hook in the other hand, no shirt, and wind tearing at everything.

| Pattern | Class | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| chain sweep | green | a wide slow arc along the roof; countering it staggers him hard |
| hook drag | red | thrown, caught, and he **hauls you toward the rear edge** |
| shoulder charge | red | straight down the roof, and he does not stop at the gap between carriages |
| **the uncouple** | — | twice in the fight he drops to the gap and pulls the pin |

**The uncouple** is the fight. The carriage behind you detaches, falls back, and **the arena's
rear wall advances 60 px**. The roof gets shorter as the fight goes on, and by the last third
there is one carriage of it with the wind pushing you toward the back. It is the chalk circle
escalated, and it runs on the arena-override feature stage 1 already needed.

**Phase two at 50 % — the bridges.** The low girders the roof section taught you start arriving
*during the fight*, on their own telegraph: a light on the horizon, then the sound. Both of you
have to duck. And his grab changes — he catches you, holds you upright, and waits. **If you do
not mash out before the girder, it takes you off the train.** That is the scariest single beat in
the chapter and it costs one timer and one existing grab.

**Death:** over the side into the dark. The relic is his coupling pin.

## Balance

**The corridor inverts the crowd problem.** Two attackers in 30 px of depth means they *queue* —
the pressure is no longer "I am surrounded", it is "I cannot get past". Launchers, the running
tackle and the wall splat all become worth much more, and enemy hp goes *down* slightly here
because you can only reach the front one.

**The roof inverts it back.** 80 px of depth, no side walls — just an edge — and wind pushing you
rearward at 0.3 px/f. Everything the corridor taught is wrong for three minutes, which is why the
level introduces no new types for it.

**Healing: 300 hp**, with two of the seven pickups in the pantry car, so the middle of the level
is where you top up and the roof is where you pay for not having.

**Checkpoints at the parcel dock, the vestibule and the ladder.** ~15 minutes for a competent
first run, 10–11 practised.

## Making it feel alive

**The board flips.** A split-flap departure board that actually changes, twice, and the second
time is your train moving to platform 1. It puts a clock on the wall with no UI.

**The station crowd is dense**, and it is the only place in the chapter that is: a slow drift
along the platform, people asleep on bedrolls who do not move at all, a queue that shuffles
forward one place every few seconds. They part around CHAD and close behind him. **Pigeons in the
girders**, forty feet up, coming down the whole height of the screen when the goods train passes.

**On the train, everything hangs and everything swings together** — chains, curtains, a plastic
bag on a hook, the light fittings. One shared sine, one phase offset each, and they swing
*before* the lurch arrives, which is how the lurch gets telegraphed without a UI.

**A family asleep in an upper berth who never wake up.** Not once, whatever happens below them.
The lair's tiger taught this: life that ignores you completely reads as real in a way reactive
life never does. One sprite, one slow breathing loop, and it will be what people remember.

**Someone's phone** two berths down, playing a tinny film song on loop for one carriage and
audible nowhere else. **The pantry is the only warm light on the train**, between two coaches of
cold blue. **The vestibule door is open** on black rushing air — the only place you can see how
fast you are going, and the TTE fight happens next to it.

**Sound:** rail joints under everything at a fixed tempo, so the whole second half has a metronome
built into its ambience, with the spacing changing on the bridge and going hard and close in the
tunnel. Announcements distorted past intelligibility except for the platform number. A horn,
twice, from somewhere ahead.

## Music

`stage2a` is the station, `stage2b` starts at the running jump. Same key and the same falling
hook, but the station's is hurried and the train's is unhurried — the level is about leaving, and
the music stops being in a rush the moment you are aboard.

The departure and the TTE run on the shared `boss` theme from stage 1. **Birju gets his own** —
`boss2` is THE 22:40 SOUTH taken to double time, so the roof sounds like the train it is on top
of.

### `stage2a` — PLATFORM 1 · 140 BPM · F♯ minor

```
90s arcade beat 'em up stage theme with a clock running through it: 140 BPM, F#
minor, urgent and busy — a breakbeat under a four-on-the-floor kick, a ticking closed hi-hat
pattern that never stops, stabbing FM brass, a hurried bansuri hook, tabla rolls, and a
recurring two-note station bell motif like a departure chime. Underneath it all, a low diesel
engine drone. Crowded, hot, impatient, somewhere you are trying to get out of. Dry punchy Sega
Genesis mixdown, Yuzo Koshiro lineage. No build-and-drop, no ambient intro, and it must loop.
```

### `stage2b` — THE 22:40 SOUTH · 140 BPM · F♯ minor

The rhythm *is* the train: ask for the rail joints as the percussion bed and let everything else
play over the top.

```
90s arcade beat 'em up night-drive theme built on a train rhythm: 140 BPM, F#
minor, the percussion bed is a steady clattering rail-joint shuffle — two hits, a gap, two hits
— under a rolling FM bassline. A lonely bansuri plays the same falling hook as the station
theme, now unhurried, with long tape delay. Warm analogue pads, a distant horn twice per loop,
brushed metallic percussion, an occasional shift where the rhythm crosses a bridge and the
reverb suddenly opens up. Nocturnal, moving, endless, more momentum than menace. Dry punchy Sega
Genesis mixdown, Yuzo Koshiro lineage. No build-and-drop, no ambient intro, and it must loop.
```

### `boss2` — THE COUPLER · 160 BPM · F♯ minor

The level boss, on the roof of the thing whose rhythm the level has been running on for eight
minutes. Take that rhythm and double it.

```
90s arcade beat 'em up boss theme on the roof of a speeding train: 160 BPM, F# minor, the
clattering rail-joint shuffle from the night theme now at double time and hammered, under a
furious distorted FM lead playing the same falling hook at twice the speed. Wind noise across
the whole track, chain-rattle percussion, a rolling acid bassline, a horn blast every sixteen
bars like something coming the other way, and a moment every loop where everything drops to just
the rails and comes back. Fast, exposed, high up, nowhere to stand. Dry punchy Sega Genesis
mixdown. No ambient intro, no breakdown, and it must loop.
```
