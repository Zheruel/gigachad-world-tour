# STAGE 3 — REFUND TOWER

> Status: unimplemented proposal. This document describes intended content, not the
> current runtime.

One route, 8000 logical px, **16.7 screens**, about **15 minutes**. The train gets in at dawn, in
the monsoon, in another city, and the chapter ends on a roof over the sea. The shortest level and
the densest: it is the one that stops being about *where you are* and starts being about *who you
hit first*.

## The areas

| | Area | x | Length | The one idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **the flooded street** | 0–1400 | ~3 min | ankle-deep water, and a live cable drifting in it |
| 2 | **the lobby and the chowki** | 1400–2600 | ~3½ min | marble under two inches of flood, and a police post in a private building |
| 3 | **the lift** | 2600–2800 | **~30 s** | no enemies. The doors close, the indicator climbs, and you can hear the floor above |
| 4 | **the call floor** | 2800–5200 | ~4 min | the lifts deliver waves into the *middle* of the arena |
| 5 | **the manager's end** | 5200–6200 | ~2 min | glass office, a PA horn on a pillar, and hold music |
| 6 | **the roof** | 6200–8000 | ~2½ min | rain, a wet deck, the sea, and no healing at all |

**The lift is this level's quiet area.** Thirty seconds: the doors shut on a flooded lobby, the
indicator counts, rain noise drops away to a hum, and through the ceiling you can hear a hundred
people on headsets. Then the doors open on a room that is dry, cold and busy. Checkpoint.

## What the building does

**Downstairs, the water.** Dashes lose half their distance and throw a bow wave, knockdowns
splash, and **a fallen live cable electrifies a patch of flood that drifts** — a hazard that
moves on its own and hurts both sides. The ground floor is about where the water is.

**Upstairs, the machinery.** Cubicle rows are breakable cover, the server aisle runs hot enough
to be a poison-system hazard, and **the lifts deliver waves into the middle of the arena** — the
one direction enemies have never come from in three levels. The first time the doors open behind
you it rewrites how the room reads.

**The light** is the level's signature against the other two: flat dead fluorescent, a rotating
police beacon in the lobby sweeping a hard shadow across everything every four seconds, and
lightning through the glass. Level 1 had hard sun and sodium. Level 2 had tungsten and a strobing
window. This one is white and cold, and the only warm light in it arrives on the roof in the last
ninety seconds, when the sun comes up.

## The cast

Two new types in the whole level, and both are about target priority. Everything else is the
roster you already know, in office clothes — which after two levels is a joke that lands on its
own.

| | Role | hp | dmg | speed | Telegraph | The answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RIOT** *(new)* | shield | 70 | 10 | 0.7 | green baton jab, 22f | never frontal: flank, launch, or parry into him |
| **TEAM LEAD** *(new)* | support | 44 | — | 1.0 | none — he never attacks | kill him first or nothing flinches |
| **GOONDA** *(swarm flag)* | swarm | 12 | 3 + flash | 1.5 | none — they mob | five at a time in lanyards, filming you |
| **THELA** | heavy | 100 | 14 | 0.6 | red grab, 30f | a server trolley instead of a cart — and he throws the swarm at you |
| **COOKER** | zoner | 30 | 8 | 0.8 | red steam, 26f | a burst coolant line; same beam, same lethal death |
| **BANDAR** | thief | 19 | 5 | 1.9 | green leap | on the roof, in the rain, forty floors up |

**RIOT** blocks everything frontal, full stop. He must be flanked, launched or parried into — and
standing behind a TEAM LEAD he is the reason this level is called priority.

**TEAM LEAD** never attacks, never blocks, walks slowly, and every enemy inside his aura gains
poise and stops flinching. As long as he is alive your hits do damage but not *control*, and
control is what a beat 'em up runs on. He is a puzzle wearing a lanyard.

**The swarm is a goonda in a lanyard** — a wave flag, not a type. Twelve hp, three damage, five
at once, and every few seconds one holds up a phone and **the flash blinds for 15 frames**. A
swarm whose threat is your vision rather than your health, answered by one wide attack or a super:
the game teaching you what supers are actually for.

**THELA's fourth prop** is a server trolley, and it comes with the best joke in the chapter — he
picks up one of the swarm and **throws them at you**. Enemy as projectile, friendly fire included.

## MINIBOSS — THE SHIELD WALL

Three RIOTs abreast across a flooded lobby, moving as one, with the swarm behind them throwing
whatever is to hand. **A formation, not a character** — because this level is about target
priority and the honest way to test that is a *shape*.

There is no way through the front. The answers are all spatial: launch one out of the line and
the wall has a hole for two seconds; drive one into the drifting live cable; or bait the swarm
forward and use the beacon sweep, which is the one moment every four seconds when you can see
exactly where all six of them are.

## MID-BOSS — THE INSPECTOR

A boss who is a *procedure*, at a desk with a ceiling fan and a wall of files, in a police chowki
inside a private lobby — which tells you everything about who he works for.

- **lathi thrust** (green, counterable) — long, straight, and he leans into it.
- **the low spin** (red) — jump it; it sweeps his own men too.
- **tear gas** (green, reflectable canister) — leaves a cloud that poisons whoever stands in it.
- **the whistle** — brings two more men. Forever. There is no wave counter and no end: the fight
  does not stop until you **break the whistle on his belt**, a small fast target with its own 12 hp
  that you can only reach while he is recovering from the lathi.

At 50 % the cap and shirt come off — **OFF DUTY** — the whistle is finished either way, and what
is left is a large man with a stick who is finally just fighting you.

## LEVEL BOSS — "SIR", THE CLOSER

The chapter's final boss, in two phases and two kinds of light.

### Phase one — the call floor

Pressed shirt, lanyard, a headset he never takes off, one hand raised to say *a moment*.

- **the handset throw** (green, reflectable) — two, then three, then four at a time.
- **the cabinet shove** (red) — a filing cabinet tipped down the lane.
- **dash punch** (green, counterable) — the only honest thing he does.
- **ON HOLD** — the mechanic. Every ~25 seconds **he takes a call**: the music ducks to hold
  muzak, a queue of the swarm forms up in front of him, and he cannot be touched behind it. Clear
  the queue, *or* **reflect a handset into him to hang up** — four seconds instead of twenty. The
  chapter has spent two levels teaching reflect; this is the exam, and the shortcut is the reward.
- **The PA horn on the pillar is breakable.** Break it and the hold music has nowhere to play: the
  queue still forms, but *silent and half the size*. Nobody will tell you this.

### Phase two — the roof

At 30 % he walks into the lift, and you take the fire stair up into the storm.

He throws the headset off the roof. No queue, no muzak, no summons — rain, a wet reflective deck,
the sea forty floors down, and a man who has never once fought his own fight being unpleasantly
good at it: fast, close, nothing over 20 frames of wind-up, and a grab that ends with you on the
parapet.

**The rain is a soft blind.** Visibility drops and the lightning is the only thing that lights him
properly, so his tell is his *silhouette* — the last thing the chapter asks you to read, and what
every boss in it has been training.

**Death:** first light, and he ends up hanging off the parapet by the wrist of the man he has been
paying to look the other way. The relic is his headset.

## The waves

Seven gates and three boss-grade fights — fewer waves than the other levels and longer boss
fights, because this one's difficulty is in composition rather than count.

| # | x | Composition | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 400 | goonda ×3, swarm ×3 | fighting in water |
| 2 | 900 | goonda ×2, swarm ×4, with the live cable | the floor is a hazard now |
| — | 1700 | **THE SHIELD WALL** | you cannot hit the front |
| 3 | 2000 | cooker, riot, goonda ×2 | chip damage from behind a shield |
| — | 2400 | **THE INSPECTOR** | find the whistle |
| 4 | 2900 | riot, goonda ×2, swarm ×3, **from the lifts** | enemies from the middle |
| 5 | 3500 | team lead, swarm ×5, goonda ×2 | nothing flinches until the lanyard dies |
| 6 | 4100 | thela, cooker, riot, swarm ×3 | poise, steam and a shield at once |
| 7 | 4700 | team lead, riot ×2, thela, cooker, swarm ×4 | the exam, with the lifts running |
| — | 5400 / 6600 | **"SIR"**, two phases | everything |

**The priority ladder is the level.** RIOT says *not the front one*; the cooker's beam says *not
from there*; TEAM LEAD says *not the one hitting you*; THELA says *not yet*. By wave 7 all four
are on screen with five of the swarm, and the correct order of execution is a real answer that a
good player finds and a masher does not.

## Balance

**The swarm is the pressure valve.** Twelve hp of nothing, five at a time, they make every combo
counter look enormous and they are why a wide super feels correct here rather than wasteful. A
level about restraint needs one thing you are allowed to be reckless with.

**Healing: 300 hp**, but two of the coolers are past a lift, so topping up costs you a wave
arriving from behind — and none of it is above the fire stair.

**Checkpoints at the shield wall, the lift and the fire-stair door**, which puts the roof about
four minutes from a retry. ~15 minutes for a competent first run.

## Making it feel alive

**The telecallers never stop working.** Three bays of them, headsets on, typing, taking calls,
through a fistfight ten feet away — ducking when something lands near them and going straight back
to it. Startle on arrival, hold for a fixed count, never on proximity. One animated set, and the
most characterful thing in the level.

**Bold at 181 px.** Three cubicle bays and eight readable monitors, not thirty. Each flickers on
**its own period** so the wall never pulses as one, and one ceiling tube fails on a long cycle.

**The flood is a mirror.** Two inches over the lobby marble, so the whole wall plane is reflected
in it and wobbling, with files, cups and a floating keyboard drifting past as y-sorted actors.

**The beacon.** One rotating blue light sweeping the entire lane every four seconds, putting a
moving hard shadow behind everything including CHAD. The cheapest drama in the chapter, and the
shield wall fight is timed against it.

**The lift indicator** starts climbing about four seconds before a wave arrives. Nobody has to
explain it; by the second time, that number is the most threatening thing on screen.

**Rain and what it lands on:** sheets of it down every window in tracks that pick up the beacon,
and on the roof a full procedural layer plus wet reflections in the deck.

**Sound:** rain on glass, a hundred simultaneous phone conversations as a bed, a call-centre gong
every time somebody closes, the server aisle's fan roar in one screen only, thunder — and on the
roof, nothing but wind and rain and the sea.

## What has to be built

**Art — four sprite families, and one of them is a costume change.**

1. **RIOT** and **TEAM LEAD** — idle, walk, attack (RIOT only), hurt, down, plus RIOT's shield
   states.
2. **THE INSPECTOR** — idle, walk, lathi, spin, gas, whistle, hurt, down, plus the OFF DUTY
   costume.
3. **"SIR"** — idle, throw, shove, dash punch, hurt, down — and **phase two generated against
   phase one as a reference image**, exactly the way the lair's bed poses were built, so it is
   unmistakably the same man soaked through.
4. Props: water cooler, cubicle partition, server rack, evidence locker, PA horn, filing cabinet.

**Code**

1. Flood: dash damping, bow waves, and the drifting live-cable hazard.
2. Lifts as a spawn source in the middle of the arena, with the indicator as the telegraph.
3. The shield: frontal immunity, flank/launch/parry-into exceptions.
4. The aura: poise granted to everything inside a radius, cleared on the lead's death.
5. ON HOLD: music duck, the queue, the reflect-to-hang-up shortcut, and the PA horn's effect on it.
6. Rain, wet-deck reflections, and the soft-blind visibility drop.

**Checks:** `shield-blocks-only-the-front`, `aura-dies-with-the-lead`, `lift-spawns-mid-arena`,
`cable-hurts-both-sides`, `hold-queue-blocks-damage`, `reflected-handset-hangs-up`,
`pa-horn-halves-the-queue`, `roof-has-no-healing`.

## Music

`stage3a` is the flooded street and the lobby, `stage3b` is everything above the lift. The shield
wall and the Inspector run on the shared `boss` theme from stage 1; **Sir gets his own** — the
chapter's last two minutes should not sound like the six fights before them. The level also needs
a piece of music that is really a **sound effect**: the hold muzak.

**The best joke in the soundtrack is that `stage3b` and `hold` share a melody.** Write the muzak
first, then ask for the office theme as that same cheerful phrase dragged into the minor and
played hard. The first time you hear the tune it is a joke; by the boss fight it means he is
untouchable.

**The fire stair has no music.** Wind and rain, thirty seconds, and then the roof.

### `stage3a` — LANDFALL · 126 BPM · C minor

```
90s arcade beat 'em up theme, monsoon dawn in a coastal Indian city: 126 BPM, C
minor, heavy and wet — a slow dub-influenced half-time beat, enormous sub bass, sparse rim
clicks, a cold four-note synth motif repeating like a rotating beacon, distant thunder, muted
brass stabs drowned in reverb, and a rain-stick shimmer across the whole track. Grey, exhausted,
arriving somewhere at the wrong hour. Dry punchy Sega Genesis mixdown under all of it. No build-and-drop, no
ambient intro, and it must loop.
```

### `hold` — PLEASE CONTINUE TO HOLD · 96 BPM · C major, on purpose

Diegetic. It plays through the PA when Sir takes a call and tinnily through the ceiling while you
ride the lift. Thirty to sixty seconds is plenty, and **this one is not dry** — it should sound
like a small blown speaker at the end of a corridor.

```
Corporate telephone hold music, deliberately cheap and awful: 96 BPM, C major, a
plinky synth marimba and a fretless bass playing an insipidly cheerful four-bar melody over a
preset bossa nova drum machine, with a thin string patch and a saxophone-preset countermelody.
Extremely repetitive, slightly out of tune, mixed as if playing through a small blown speaker at
the end of a corridor — band-limited, boxy, no low end, a faint hiss. It should be mildly
irritating and it must loop seamlessly.
```

### `stage3b` — THE FLOOR · 144 BPM · C minor

```
90s arcade beat 'em up theme, a sickly corporate melody turned aggressive: 144 BPM,
C minor, a cheerful major-key hold-music phrase dragged into the minor and played hard on a
detuned FM lead over a relentless machine beat. Clinical arpeggiated synths, clattering
industrial percussion like a room full of keyboards, cold fluorescent pads, a low pulsing drone,
and a telephone ring tone used as a rhythmic element. Fluorescent, airless, unpleasantly
cheerful underneath the aggression. Dry punchy Sega Genesis mixdown, Yuzo Koshiro lineage. No build-and-drop, no
ambient intro, and it must loop.
```

### `final` — THE CLOSER · 156 BPM · C minor

Both of Sir's phases. It has to feel like a different category of thing from the shared `boss`
theme, not just faster.

```
90s arcade FINAL BOSS theme, rain on a rooftop at dawn: 156 BPM, C minor, colder
and grander than a normal boss theme. A driving industrial beat under enormous detuned FM brass
in a slow, inevitable four-chord descent, a screaming lead over the top, choir-like synth pads

```
